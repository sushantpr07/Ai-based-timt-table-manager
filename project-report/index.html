01 · Project Overview

What is Dayleaf?

Page 2 of 18

Dayleaf is an AI timetable manager built for universities and engineering colleges. The institution enters its departments, subjects, teachers, classrooms and labs once — Dayleaf then computes a complete weekly timetable where no room, teacher or student section is ever double-booked. Teachers and students sign in to view their personal real-time schedules.

Automate

Timetable in seconds

A mathematical constraint solver (Google OR-Tools CP-SAT) places every lecture and lab into a valid slot — replacing weeks of spreadsheet work.

Assist

Built-in AI Copilot

Dayleaf AI (Google Gemini) answers questions in plain English, like "find a free room on Wednesday afternoon", and prepares verified timetable drafts.

Access

One portal, every role

Role-based workspaces for College Admin, HOD, Faculty and Students — each person sees and controls only what their role permits.

02 · Real-World Need Today

Why colleges need Dayleaf in today's date

Page 3 of 18

Problems in today's manual scheduling

  • Timetables are still built manually in Excel — taking coordinators 2 to 3 weeks at the start of every semester.
  • Frequent clashes: two batches assigned to the same computer lab, or one professor scheduled in two halls simultaneously.
  • One change breaks the whole week — a single faculty change or new section forces hours of manual re-checking.
  • High-tech labs sit idle while lecture halls overflow, because room utilization is never measured.
  • Students and teachers get updates late via scattered WhatsApp forwards and notice-board printouts.
  • NEP 2020 multidisciplinary courses and growing batch sizes have made manual scheduling mathematically intractable.

03 · Design Thinking Methodology

How we applied the 5 stages of Design Thinking

Page 4 of 18
1

Empathize

Interviewed students, faculty, HODs and timetable coordinators about daily scheduling pain points.

2

Define

Defined the core problem: manual timetables are slow, error-prone and opaque. Users need clash-free automation with human control.

3

Ideate

Evaluated spreadsheet macros, greedy heuristics, and constraint programming + AI. Selected OR-Tools CP-SAT + Gemini AI.

4

Prototype

Engineered Dayleaf: Next.js dark-mode studio, FastAPI backend, CP-SAT solver, Gemini assistant and role-based portals.

5

Test

Seeded a full 4-department campus, published a 33-session master timetable and verified 50/50 automated API test suites.

What campus stakeholders told us (Empathize)

  • College Admin: "Every semester I spend weeks fixing cross-department clashes in Excel."
  • Department HOD: "Shared computer labs get double-booked between CSE and Cybersecurity batches."
  • Faculty Member: "Some days I get 4 back-to-back lectures without a break, and other days long empty gaps."
  • B.Tech Student: "We walk to a lab and find another section already inside."

Key design decisions (Define → Ideate)

  • Hard constraints that can never be violated (rooms, faculty, sections, seat capacity, lab equipment).
  • Soft constraints to optimize human comfort — balanced daily load and fewer idle gaps.
  • Conversational AI so staff can ask questions in plain language.
  • Draft → Review → Publish workflow so administrators always have final approval.

Hard constraints — strictly enforced
  • At most one session per room per time slot
  • A teacher cannot be in two rooms at once
  • A student section cannot have overlapping classes
  • Room capacity must be ≥ section student count
  • Lab courses scheduled only in laboratory rooms
  • Faculty availability windows & breaks respected
Soft constraints — optimized
  • Even distribution of sessions across weekdays
  • Minimized idle gaps for sections and teachers
  • Weekly teaching load cap per faculty (default 18)
  • Lower soft-penalty score = higher schedule quality
Gemini AI Copilot

Translates natural-language prompts into structured queries or draft generation — still validated by the CP-SAT solver.

B.Tech CSE (Cyber Security) Engineering

Defensive security controls implemented

  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Every endpoint verifies the user's role and `institution_id` so students/faculty can never modify campus records.
  • HTTP-Only Session & Refresh Cookies: JWT tokens are stored in `HttpOnly` cookies (inaccessible to XSS scripts) with rotating refresh token families.
  • CSRF Double-Submit HMAC: All state-changing requests (`POST`, `PUT`, `PATCH`, `DELETE`) verify `X-CSRF-Token` using constant-time `hmac.compare_digest`.
  • Password Hashing & Rate Limiting: Passwords hashed via `pwdlib` (Argon2/recommended); authentication routes protected against brute-force attempts.

Project Conclusion

Through the five stages of Design Thinking (Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test), our team identified a universal problem in higher education and engineered Dayleaf — a complete, working AI timetable manager.

  • Reduces timetable preparation from weeks of manual Excel work to seconds.
  • Guarantees zero room, faculty or section clashes through mathematical constraint solving.
  • Makes campus planning accessible through a natural-language Gemini AI Copilot.
  • Delivers dedicated, secure portals for Admins, HODs, Faculty and Students.
  • Embeds Cyber Security best practices (RBAC, CSRF HMAC, HTTP-only cookies, audit logs).
Dayleaf transforms university timetable management from a stressful manual bottleneck into an intelligent, transparent and clash-free system.
